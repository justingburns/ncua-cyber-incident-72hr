2. Reportability determination

Outcome: REPORTABLE. A substantial cyber incident that meets Prongs A, B and C.

Assessed Thu Nov 12, 2026 6:17 PM PST by pexample.

TestAnswerRationaleCitation
Is it a cyber incident?YesRansomware encrypted the core processing environment our provider hosts for us. Member account data and core availability were actually jeopardized without lawful authority.12 CFR 748.1(c)(2) (definition of cyber incident)
Is it excluded as authorized, good-faith activity?NoNo testing or other authorized activity was under way. The provider confirmed a criminal intrusion.12 CFR 748.1(c)(1)(ii)
Is the cyber incident substantial?YesCore, online banking and card authorizations were unavailable for more than four hours. NCUA's Appendix A lists ransomware on critical systems as an example that likely qualifies.12 CFR 748.1(c)(1)(i); 23-CU-07 Appendices A and B
Prong A: substantial loss of confidentiality, integrity or availabilityYesVital member services (online banking, bill pay, shared branching) were disrupted. Exposure of member data is still being investigated.12 CFR 748.1(c)(1)(i)(A)
Prong B: disruption from a cyberattack or exploited vulnerabilityYesThe disruption resulted from a cyberattack (ransomware).12 CFR 748.1(c)(1)(i)(B)
Prong C: third-party or supply chain compromiseYesThe disruption was caused by a compromise of our third-party core processing and data hosting provider.12 CFR 748.1(c)(1)(i)(C); 23-CU-07
